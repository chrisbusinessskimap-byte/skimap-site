In brief

Ski Maps works on your phone. Your GPS position does not leave the device: it is used to draw the “you are here” dot, to snap to the piste and to calculate routes, all locally. What can be sent is the diagnostics, which you choose; closed-piste reports, which you decide to send; and, during a navigation, lift waiting times and leg times, always without your position, which you can switch off in Settings with “Automatic contributions”. If you create an account, your user ID and email also reach the server (see “The account”).

Your location

The location permission is used for navigation. Coordinates stay in memory and on the phone: they are not saved in any remote archive and do not appear in any data sent. If you refuse the permission, the map and route calculation remain usable by choosing the starting point by hand.

Diagnostics (optional, off by default)

On first launch the app asks whether to turn them on: until you say yes, no identifier is written and no event is sent. The code is only created when needed: when you consent, or when you send your first report. If you turn them on, the app sends technical events about how route calculation works, for one reason only: to find the points of the ski area it cannot connect, and to fix the map. You can turn them off at any time in Settings.

There are five events: you chose a destination; a route was requested; a route was not found, with the reason; a recalculation was requested; you strayed from the route. Each one carries:

  • a random code generated by the app, the same for all submissions from this phone, plus a code for the individual session;
  • app version, operating system, ski area and map version;
  • the codes of the points and sections of the ski area involved, the level chosen, the reason for the failure, how many routes were found;
  • how far you were from the route, in bands (less than 50 m, 50–100, 100–250, more) and not in exact metres.

No coordinates, no contacts, no phone or advertising identifier, no tracking across apps or sites.

Pseudonymous, not anonymous

The random code contains nothing about you and is not linked to a name or an email address, but it stays the same over time: a series of events with the same code can be traced back to the same installation. That is why diagnostics are asked for first, rather than taken for granted. [DA CONFERMARE: vale ancora se l’utente ha un account?]

Closed-piste reports

When you report a piste, three pieces of information reach the server: the ski area, the section and the status (open, closed, don’t know). Not your phone’s clock time, not your position, not the diagnostics code. Who voted is established by the server, through an anonymous identity created at your first vote: nobody looking at the votes can trace them back to you. Reading other people’s votes does not create any identity. [DA CONFERMARE: l’utenza anonima resta separata dall’account o si unisce quando accedi?]

How long you wait at lifts

The app times by itself how long you stand at the start of a lift before you begin to go up, and sends the server four pieces of information: which lift, how many minutes, in which half-hour of the day and which day of the week it was. Not the exact time, not your position, not the route you are following. It is used to tell other skiers how long they will really wait, which is the one thing no public source knows: queue time exists nowhere unless the people skiing measure it. Like closed-piste reports, it is a contribution to the map and not diagnostics, so it is not covered by the diagnostics switch: it has its own, “Automatic contributions” in Settings, on until you switch it off. Switched off, the app measures nothing and sends nothing, not even what it had already measured. Measurements that look wrong — more than half an hour, or with a lost GPS signal — are discarded on the phone and never sent.

How long each leg takes

During a navigation the app compares, leg by leg, the time it had estimated with the time you really took, and sends the server: the type of leg (the difficulty of the descent or the type of lift), the length rounded to 10 metres, the level chosen, the estimated and actual minutes, and the time of day the leg began. Not your position, not the names or codes of the pistes, not the exact time, not the route. It is used to make the times the app tells everyone more accurate. Legs with a long pause, a GPS gap or an out-of-range time are discarded on the phone and never sent. They are sent together at the end of the navigation, with the same anonymous identity as the reports, and are switched off with the same switch as the waiting times, “Automatic contributions” in Settings.

Voice guidance

If voice guidance is on, the navigation phrases (“in 150 metres keep right”) are spoken by the phone’s speech synthesiser: Ski Maps records nothing, listens to nothing and does not send those phrases to any server of its own. It must be said, however, who that synthesiser is: on Android it is usually Google’s, and depending on the phone’s settings a voice may be generated online — in that case the phrase, which contains the name of the piste or lift, goes through the servers of whoever supplies the voice. From the app it is not possible to know which voices work over the network and which on the phone: the system does not say. If you prefer to avoid this, voice guidance can be switched off in Settings, and with the voice off nothing is sent.

With the phone in your pocket

During a navigation, and only then, Ski Maps keeps using your location even with the screen off or another app open: that is what lets the guidance follow you with the phone in your pocket. Android makes this visible with a fixed notification, “Navigating to…”, which stays as long as the navigation is active. It stops on arrival, when you tap “End” or “I’m exploring”, when you close the app, and in any case after 8 hours. On phones with Android 13 or later, the first time you start a navigation you are asked for the notification permission: it is only used to show that one. Coordinates stay on the phone as always: they are not saved in any remote archive and are not sent with any data.

Who else sees something

As with any app that uses the network, the services contacted see your IP address:

  • Supabase (database): receives diagnostic events, votes, waiting times and leg times;
  • Cloudflare R2: the app downloads from here the route map and the status of the ski areas and, if you turn on the satellite view, the winter photo: in that case it sees which areas you look at;
  • MapTiler: serves the background maps, so it sees which areas you look at and which ones you download for offline use. If its map is not available, the app falls back on OpenStreetMap, with the same effect;
  • Google, only if you choose to sign in with Google: verifies your identity and sees that you are signing in to Ski Maps.

These steps do not depend on diagnostics: they are needed to make the map and the account work. No data is sold, or used for profiling or advertising. Supabase, Cloudflare and MapTiler process this data on behalf of the controller: they are processors and do not decide what to do with it. How long data is kept

Nothing stays on the server forever: every night it deletes by itself whatever has expired. Diagnostic events are kept for 12 months, which covers a whole season and the summer in which the map is corrected. Votes on pistes are shown in the app for 12 hours and stay on the phone for the same time; they are deleted from the server after 30 days, waiting times after 60, leg times after 12 months, because they are used to compare a whole season. The anonymous identity for reports, waiting times and leg times is deleted after 12 months without use.

Your rights

You can withdraw consent at any time, in Settings, and with “Automatic contributions” you can object to the measurements the app takes by itself during navigation. With “Delete my data” the app deletes from the phone the random code, the events still waiting to be sent, the saved votes, the times not yet sent and the anonymous identity for reports: on restart you are asked again what you prefer. What has already reached the server stays there until it expires. To find out which diagnostic events exist, or to have them deleted sooner, write to the address below: the reply arrives within a month. To find them we need the random code, which you can find in Settings: write it down before deleting it. Reports, waiting times and leg times, on the other hand, are not linked to that code, and we have no way of knowing which ones are yours: they delete themselves when they expire. You can also lodge a complaint with the Italian Data Protection Authority (Garante per la protezione dei dati personali) or with the supervisory authority of the country where you live.

For account data you can ask us to delete it (how to do it). [DA CONFERMARE: accesso, correzione e portabilità dei dati dell’account: modalità e tempi da riportare]

This does not concern your data, but it must be said here. The Ski Maps route map is built from OpenStreetMap: it is a derived database, distributed under the ODbL 1.0 licence — © OpenStreetMap contributors, data modified. The relief that says which way each piste runs downhill comes from two terrain models:

  • INGV TINITALY (CC BY 4.0): Tarquini S., I. Isola, M. Favalli, A. Battistini, G. Dotta (2023). TINITALY, a digital elevation model of Italy with a 10 meters cell size (Version 1.1). Istituto Nazionale di Geofisica e Vulcanologia (INGV). https://doi.org/10.13127/tinitaly/1.1;

Ski bus and train timetables come from the open data of STA Südtirolmobil and Trentino Trasporti; season dates from Open Data Hub Südtirol.
