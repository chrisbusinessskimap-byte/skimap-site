Privacy

What stays on your phone, what leaves it.

The same notice you find in the app, under Settings → Privacy notice. Updated 17 October 2026 · version 6.

In short

Ski Maps works on the phone. GPS coordinates draw the “you are here” dot, snap you to the piste and work out routes, all locally, and they leave the phone in one case only: if you say yes to “Piste tracks”, a separate question. What can be sent is diagnostics, which you choose; closed-piste reports, which you decide to send; waiting times at lifts and, while navigating, leg times, which you choose the first time you open the app and can turn off in You with “Automatic contributions”; and, only if you say yes, piste tracks. Reports, times and tracks say where you were, but next to them there is only a pseudonym that changes every day. If you create an account, which is optional, your email address and three preferences are sent too.

Your position

Location permission is used for navigation. Without a yes to tracks, coordinates stay in memory and on the phone: they are not stored in any remote archive and do not appear in any data sent. With a yes, the points recorded on pistes and lifts are sent, as the section on tracks explains. Some data that is sent still says where you were indirectly — a vote on which piste, a waiting time at which lift — and that is why it carries a pseudonym that changes every day, not your user. If you refuse the permission, the map and route planning still work by choosing the start by hand.

Diagnostics (optional, off by default)

If you turn it on, the app sends technical events about how route calculation behaves. It serves one purpose only: finding the points of the ski area the app cannot connect, and fixing the map. You can turn it off whenever you like in Settings; from that moment nothing is collected and nothing is sent.

There are five events:

  • you chose a destination
  • a route was requested
  • a route was not found, with the reason
  • a recalculation was requested
  • you moved away from the route

Each event carries:

  • a random code generated by the app, the same for every send from this phone (it tells a problem that repeats apart from problems of ten different people), plus a code for the single session
  • app version, operating system, ski area and map version
  • the codes of the points and sections of the ski area involved — the destination and, if you picked it by hand, the start: never the point or run you are on —, the level chosen, the reason for the failure, how many routes were found
  • how far you were from the route, in bands (under 50 m, 50–100, 100–250, more) and not in exact metres

No coordinates, no contact details, no device or advertising identifier, no tracking across apps or sites.

Pseudonymous, not anonymous

The random code contains nothing about you and is not linked to a name or an email address, but it stays the same over time: a series of events with the same code can be traced back to the same installation. That is why diagnostics are asked for beforehand instead of being taken for granted, and why the code is created only when it is needed: when you consent to diagnostics, or when you send your first report. If you say no and report nothing, it is never written to the phone. The code is not linked to your account either, if you create one.

Closed-run reports

When you report a run, three pieces of information reach the server: the ski area, the section and the state (open, closed, not sure). Not your phone’s clock, not your position, not your diagnostics code. Who votes is decided by the server, through an identity created at your first vote (anonymous, or your account if you have one). That identity is not stored next to the vote, though: what is stored is a pseudonym that changes every day, derived from it with a key that is deleted as soon as the day ends. It is used for the limits against abuse; from the next day nobody, not even us, can trace a vote back to you or to your account. Reading other people’s votes creates no identity. Reports serve other skiers, and for that reason they are not covered by the diagnostics switch: you send them because you choose to vote.

How long the lift queues are

The app times by itself how long you stay at the bottom of a lift before you start going up — at every lift you take while using it, not only while navigating — and sends the server: which lift, how many minutes, which half hour of the day and which day of the week it was, and how long ago it measured it. That last one is for “live” waits, those of the last 45 minutes, which the app shows before those of other days: with it the server knows what time the queue was. Not your position, not the route you are on. It tells other skiers how long they will really wait, which is the one thing no public source knows. Like closed-piste reports, it is a contribution to the map and not diagnostics: it has its own switch, “Automatic contributions”, which you choose the first time you open the app and can change any time in You. Off, the app measures nothing and sends nothing, not even what it had already measured. Measurements that look wrong — more than half an hour, less than half a minute, or with imprecise GPS — are thrown away on the phone and never sent. A measurement says which lift you were at and when: that is why it only carries the day’s pseudonym, never your user or your account.

How long each leg takes

During navigation the app compares, leg by leg, the time it had estimated with the time you actually took, and sends the server: the kind of leg (the difficulty of the run or the type of lift), the length rounded to 10 metres, the level you chose, the estimated and the real minutes, and the hour of the day the leg started. Not your position, not the names or codes of the runs, not the exact time, not the route. It makes the times the app gives everyone more accurate. Legs with a long stop, a GPS gap or a time out of scale are thrown away on the phone and never leave it. They are sent together at the end of navigation, with the pseudonym of the day, like reports, and are switched off by the same switch as waiting times, “Automatic contributions” in Settings.

Piste tracks (only if you say yes)

This is the only case in which coordinates leave the phone, and it happens only if you said yes to the question on the first screen, or in You. When you navigate, when you record your day and when you keep the map open, the app snaps your position to pistes and lifts and sends the server: the pistes and lifts you rode, in what order, with the seconds of entry and exit, stops and the queue before each lift; and the GPS points (latitude, longitude, accuracy, speed, altitude, time) recorded ON pistes and lifts, with the map version and the chosen level. Points away from the pistes — the town, the hotel, the car park, the bus — are not sent, nor are the first and last 2 minutes of each recording. They carry the day’s pseudonym and a random key that is new every day, which links the recordings of the same day but not two days together: not your account, not the diagnostics code. They are used to work out how long each piste really takes and how long you wait at each lift, to say how crowded the pistes are hour by hour and to understand how people move around the ski area. The figures derived from them may be shared with lift companies, only in aggregate form and never below 5 people. A track with times is still personal data, even if pseudonymous: that is why your yes is needed, and why you can turn it off in You whenever you like. The legal basis is consent (art. 6.1.a GDPR).

“Record your day”, in Contribute, keeps GPS on even in your pocket and at the end shows runs, km, vertical, lifts and minutes queuing: these statistics stay on the phone. It stops with “Stop”, after 8 hours, when you change region or after an hour away from the pistes. It also works without a yes to tracks: then the day is not sent.

Your account (optional)

You can use Ski Maps without an account. If you create one — with your email address, to which we send a 6-digit code instead of a password, or with Google — we keep on the server your email address, when the account was created and last used, and the IP address seen by the sign-in service. With Google, Supabase also stores what Google sends along with the sign-in: name, profile picture and email address; Ski Maps does not use or show them. The account is there so you can find yourself again on another phone with your settings, and for nothing else: it is not linked to diagnostics, to your position, to your reports or to your waiting times. The legal basis is the performance of the service you ask for by creating the account (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Settings that follow your account

With an account, three settings are also saved on the server, so you find them on every phone you sign in from: your skill level, the map settings (satellite, “My runs”, hidden place categories) and whether voice guidance is on. For each one we also save when you changed it, to keep the most recent. The saved car park and recent searches are NOT saved: they say where you have been, and they stay on the phone.

Voice guidance

When voice guidance is on, the navigation phrases (“in 150 metres keep right”) are spoken by the phone’s speech synthesiser: Ski Maps records nothing, listens to nothing, and sends those phrases to no server of its own. It is worth saying who that synthesiser is, though: on Android it is usually Google’s, and depending on the phone’s settings a voice may be generated online — in which case the phrase, which contains the name of the run or the lift, passes through the voice provider’s servers. The app cannot tell which voices work over the network and which on the device: the system does not say. If you would rather avoid it, voice guidance can be switched off in Settings, and with the voice off nothing leaves the phone.

With the phone in your pocket

While navigating and while recording your day, and only then, Ski Maps keeps using your location with the screen off or another app open: that is what lets guidance follow you with the phone in your pocket. Android makes it visible with a permanent notification, “Navigating to…” or “Recording your day”, which stays while navigation or recording is on. It stops when you arrive, when you tap “End”, “I’m exploring” or “Stop”, when you close the app, and in any case after 8 hours. On phones with Android 13 or later, the first time you are asked for notification permission: it is only used to show that notification. Coordinates stay on the phone, except for track points if you said yes to tracks.

Who else sees something

As with any app that uses the network, the services that are contacted see your IP address:

  • Supabase (database): receives diagnostic events, votes, waiting times, leg times and, if you said yes, piste tracks; and, if you create an account, your email address and synced preferences
  • Cloudflare R2: this is where the app downloads the route map and the ski-area status and, if you turn on the satellite, the winter photo: in that case it sees which areas you look at
  • MapTiler: serves the background maps, so it sees which areas you look at and which you download for offline use. If its map is unavailable, the app falls back to OpenStreetMap maps, with the same effect
  • Brevo: sends the email with the code to sign in to your account, so it receives your email address
  • Google: only if you choose “Continue with Google”. It lets you pick your Google account on the phone and confirms to us who you are; for its own sign-in it is an independent controller, with its own privacy notice

These steps do not depend on diagnostics: they are what makes the map and, if you create one, the account work. No data is sold, or used for profiling or advertising. Supabase, Cloudflare, MapTiler and Brevo process this data on behalf of the controller: they are data processors and do not decide what happens to it.

For how long

Nothing stays on the server for ever: every night whatever has expired is deleted automatically. Diagnostic events are kept 12 months, covering a whole season and the summer in which the map is fixed. Piste votes are shown in the app for 12 hours and stay on the phone for the same time; on the server they are deleted after 30 days, waiting times after 60, leg times after 12 months. Of tracks, GPS points are deleted after 12 months and piste passages after 24, because they are used to compare two seasons; the figures derived from them (times and crowding per piste, never below 5 people) no longer concern anyone and are kept. The anonymous user is deleted after 12 months without use; the account, if you create one, stays until you delete it and is deleted automatically after 24 months without use, together with synced preferences. The key that acts as the pseudonym for votes, times and tracks is deleted every night, as soon as the day ends.

Your rights

You can withdraw consent any time in You: to diagnostics and to tracks with their switches, and with “Automatic contributions” you can object to the measurements the app makes by itself. With “Delete my data” the app removes from the phone the random code, the events still waiting to be sent, saved votes, times and tracks not yet sent, the pending navigation and the offline route maps, and turns tracks off; it asks the server to delete ALL tracks sent from this phone, including those of earlier days (the phone knows their keys); and, if you have no account, it asks the server (as soon as there is a connection) to delete the anonymous user and the votes, waiting times and leg times sent today — those of earlier days can no longer be linked to you. If you have an account, you are signed out: the account stays, and “Delete account” (in You → Account) deletes it from the server straight away together with synced preferences and today’s contributions. On restart you are asked again what you prefer. The rest of what has already reached the server stays there until it expires. For a copy of your account data write to chris.business.skimap@gmail.com from the account’s address. To find out which diagnostic events exist, or to have them deleted earlier, write to chris.business.skimap@gmail.com: you will get an answer within a month. To find them the random code is needed, which you find in Settings: note it down before deleting it. Reports, waiting times and leg times, instead, are not linked to that code nor, from the next day, to any user: we have no way to know which are yours, and they are deleted automatically when they expire. You can also lodge a complaint with the Italian data protection authority (garanteprivacy.it) or the supervisory authority of the country where you live.

This website

This website (skimaps.cc) does not use cookies and has no analytics or tracking tools. Fonts and scripts are served by the site itself, not by other sites. Data sources and licences

This does not concern your data, but it must be said here. The Ski Maps route map is built from OpenStreetMap: it is a derived database, distributed under the ODbL 1.0 licence — © OpenStreetMap contributors, data modified. The relief that says which way each piste runs downhill comes from two terrain models:

  • INGV TINITALY (CC BY 4.0): Tarquini S., I. Isola, M. Favalli, A. Battistini, G. Dotta (2023). TINITALY, a digital elevation model of Italy with a 10 meters cell size (Version 1.1). Istituto Nazionale di Geofisica e Vulcanologia (INGV). https://doi.org/10.13127/tinitaly/1.1;
  • Copernicus EU-DEM: produced with data from the European Union’s Copernicus service. The data have been modified. The European Union is not responsible for this product and does not endorse it.

Ski bus and train timetables come from the open data of STA Südtirolmobil and Trentino Trasporti; season dates from Open Data Hub Südtirol. The cartographic background is by MapTiler; the winter satellite photo is by Copernicus Sentinel-2 (Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2026).
